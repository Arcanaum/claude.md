What changed and why

Every change answers a round table finding. Nothing was invented to fill a gap: where the business has not published a fact, the new page leaves it out.

Ordering

Before"Order now", "Contact us", "Order your shoot" and two more buttons had no link. Seven card links pointed at pages that do not exist.

AfterOne order form on the page: property address, date, services, lighting style and contact details. Every button and menu item lands on a real section.

WhyAll three reviewers ranked this first. A visitor ready to book could not.

First screen

Before"Your Listing From Every Angle" over a photograph dulled by a grey layer. No mention of photography or Los Angeles.

After"Real estate photography, Los Angeles", who it is for, what can be ordered, and the FAA licence line, beside a photograph at full brightness.

WhyA photography company's first impression was a muted photograph, and search engines had no words to match.

Claims

Before"Five times more viewers" three times with no source. Counters that read 0x, 0° and 0% until a script ran. "World-renowned", "highly acclaimed".

AfterRemoved. The FAA licence statement stays because it is a checkable fact.

WhyUnsupported numbers and broken counters read as padding and cost trust.

Leftover template content

BeforeAn Instagram block showing the builder's "This is an example" notice, four 2019 sample blog posts in the menu, and Before/After copy about LED light fittings.

AfterNone of it is on the page. The menu holds only Work, Photography, Styles, Tours and the order button.

WhyEach one tells an agent the site is unfinished.

Photographs

BeforeTours and video shown as near-black tiles with faded images and stock-looking people. A ragged portfolio grid with gaps.

AfterOnly the company's own property photographs, large and bright, on an even grid. The lighting styles are shown on the same room so the difference is visible.

WhyThe photographs are the strongest sales asset on the site; the old layout hid them.

Type and legibility

BeforeSeven font families on one page, pale grey labels around 10px.

AfterOne family. Running text at 16px and larger, with the logo green darkened so text on it passes contrast.

WhyMixed fonts looked unconsidered, and the small grey text was hard to read on a phone.

Search and accessibility

BeforeTitle "Go360Listing", no description, the logo as the share image, 33 of 51 images without alt text, the name spelled three ways.

AfterA title and description naming the service and city, a property photograph as the share image, alt text on every content photograph, one spelling: 360Listing.

WhySearch engines, link previews and screen readers had almost nothing to work with.

Length

Before11 screens on a desktop, 15 on a phone, with the first concrete service list several screens down.

After9 screens on a desktop, 13 on a phone, one idea per section, services named on the first screen.

WhyAn agent comparing vendors gives a page about a minute.

Round two: what the reviewers still want

The same three seats reviewed the redesign. Four of their points were fixed straight away; the rest are open.

Fixed after round two

  • On phones, the four kinds of photography showed names only. Their descriptions now show.
  • The FAA card on the first screen was labelled "Aerial photography" over an interior. It now reads "Drone pilots".
  • Three awkward sentences were rewritten, and a coverage promise the current site never made was removed.
  • A stray arrow after the phone number is gone.

Still open

  • The order form is not connected to an inbox. Until it is, the phone number is the only working contact.
  • The email address from the current footer is not on the new page. Two reviewers want it back; it is a one-line change once you confirm the address to publish.
  • The first screen is calmer than the old full-screen photograph. One reviewer found it less memorable and would run the photograph wider.
  • Spacing between sections is generous, most of all around the large statement about tours. One reviewer would tighten it.
  • For search: a confirmed web address, and business details in a form search engines read, to be added at launch.

Needed from the business

The redesign leaves these out because the current site does not state them.

  • Prices or packages. The page says "Price on request" until then.
  • Turnaround time and the exact service area.
  • Where orders should be sent. The form is not connected to an inbox yet.
  • Testimonials or named clients, if they may be shown.
  • Which edit each sample photograph is. The Traditional, Modern and Hybrid labels on the three samples are my reading of the gallery pages.
  • Larger floor plan files (the biggest on the site is 750 pixels wide), tour and video files, and a logo that works on a light background.
  • Privacy and terms pages, and the social accounts to link.

Limits of this comparison

  • The round table was three Claude reviewers with different briefs, not three different AI providers.
  • Scores are reviewers' judgements from screenshots and page text, not measured bookings or traffic.
  • Only the homepage was redesigned. The gallery, contact and blog pages are untouched.
  • The redesign passed its automated layout and copy checks at desktop and phone widths after the last edits. It has not been tested on real phones, and the order form has not been submitted end to end.
  • The redesign is a static page. Moving it onto the live Squarespace site is a separate job.
